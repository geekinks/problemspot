# Problem Validation Policy

## Purpose

ProblemSpot separates visibility, community support, evidence review, and
verification. A popular report is not automatically true, and a verified
problem is not automatically the highest priority.

AI and coding agents must not categorize, rank, moderate, validate, approve,
reject, or verify a problem.

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Submitted | The report was received but not reviewed. |
| Under Review | Completeness, safety, scope, and possible duplicates are being checked. |
| Needs More Information | Review cannot continue without specific context or evidence. |
| Community-Supported | People have confirmed experience, expressed support, or added useful evidence. |
| Evidence-Reviewed | An authorized reviewer assessed the available evidence. |
| Verified | A qualified human reviewer found sufficient evidence that the stated problem exists as described. |
| Disputed | Material evidence or interpretation is contested. |
| Merged | The report was combined with a canonical report while preserving attribution. |
| Rejected | The report is unsafe, out of scope, fabricated, or cannot meet published requirements. |
| Closed | Work will not continue, with a recorded reason. |

`In Progress` and `Resolved` describe solution work and do not replace the
validation statuses above.

## Validation criteria

A report may be verified when an authorized reviewer can establish:

1. A specific problem and affected group are clearly described.
2. The report is within ProblemSpot's scope and is not an emergency request.
3. Available evidence is relevant, lawful to use, and sufficiently credible.
4. Material contradictions have been addressed or recorded.
5. Sensitive claims have received an appropriate level of review.
6. The decision and its limitations can be explained without exposing private data.

Evidence may include direct observations, multiple independent confirmations,
photos or documents with permission, reliable public records, domain-expert
review, or findings from a trusted partner. No single evidence type is always
required or sufficient.

## Review process

1. Check safety, completeness, scope, and possible duplicates.
2. Request clarification without forcing unnecessary personal disclosure.
3. Separate personal confirmation from general support.
4. Review evidence provenance, relevance, recency, and limitations.
5. Record the decision, reviewer, timestamp, reason, and applicable evidence.
6. Notify the submitter and followers without exposing restricted material.

Reviewers must not validate reports where they have an undisclosed conflict of
interest. High-risk allegations should require a second reviewer when feasible.

## Duplicate merging

Only authorized humans may merge reports. Preserve each submitter's
attribution, confirmations, evidence provenance, and status history. A merge
must identify the canonical report and provide a reason.

## Disputes and appeals

A submitter or materially affected participant may request review with new
evidence or a specific procedural concern. A different reviewer should handle
the appeal where feasible. Record the result and do not silently rewrite the
original decision.

## Solution Lab gate

The AI Solution Lab becomes available only when the authoritative problem
status is `verified`. Community support, vote thresholds, or generated output
cannot unlock it.
