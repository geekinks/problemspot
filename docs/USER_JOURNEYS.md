# ProblemSpot User Journeys

User flows describe system steps. These journeys describe the motivation,
questions, trust needs, and successful outcome behind those steps.

## Journey 1: Amina reports a recurring community problem

**Context:** Amina experiences a recurring accessibility barrier. Previous
complaints disappeared without updates. She wants action but does not want her
home address or identity exposed.

| Stage | Amina's question | Product response | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | “Is this another complaint box?” | Explain report → validate → open mission → solution. | She understands the difference in under a minute. |
| Decide | “Will reporting put me at risk?” | Explain visibility, broad location, evidence access, and anonymous attribution. | She can make an informed privacy choice. |
| Report | “Can I explain this without technical language?” | Short structured form with examples and saved input. | She submits successfully on mobile. |
| Gather support | “Does anyone else experience this?” | Shareable page, confirmation, support, evidence, and following. | Relevant people add meaningful context. |
| Review | “What is happening now?” | Visible status history, requests, reasons, and notifications. | She never has to guess the current state. |
| Outcome | “Did reporting matter?” | Show mission, solution activity, testing, and impact updates. | She sees accountable progress or a clear closure reason. |

**Main failure to avoid:** silence after submission.

## Journey 2: Chidi confirms a problem without overstating evidence

**Context:** Chidi recognizes the issue but may support it without personally
experiencing it.

| Stage | Chidi's question | Product response | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | “Is this relevant to my area?” | Search and filters with safe location detail. | He finds the report quickly. |
| Understand | “What has actually been established?” | Separate claim, evidence summary, support, and status. | He does not mistake popularity for proof. |
| Act | “Which response represents me?” | Distinct confirmation, support, follow, and evidence actions. | He selects an honest action. |
| Return | “What changed?” | Meaningful, controllable notifications. | He returns for review or testing updates. |

**Main failure to avoid:** one generic vote that confuses experience, support,
and verification.

## Journey 3: Maya finds a worthwhile open-source project

**Context:** Maya can build software quickly with modern tools but struggles to
find validated problems, affected-user context, and projects where her work can
create measurable value.

| Stage | Maya's question | Product response | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | “Is this problem real and ready?” | Mission directory contains only verified problems. | She trusts the starting context. |
| Evaluate | “Do my skills fit?” | Show constraints, open questions, needed skills, proposals, and activity. | She can choose work intentionally. |
| Join | “How do I contribute without duplicating effort?” | Existing teams, roles, repositories, issues, and joining process. | She finds a scoped entry point. |
| Build | “What should we make and how will we test it?” | Human-led proposal review, milestones, success criteria, and optional Solution Lab. | Work becomes small, reviewable issues. |
| Validate solution | “Does this work for affected people?” | Community testing and documented feedback. | The team changes course when evidence requires it. |
| Demonstrate impact | “Can I show meaningful contribution?” | Reviewed attribution and outcome evidence on her profile. | Her portfolio shows impact, not commit volume. |

**Main failure to avoid:** a list of vague problems with no owner, context,
acceptance criteria, or community feedback.

## Journey 4: Ibrahim reviews a sensitive report

**Context:** Ibrahim is an authorized reviewer. He must make a defensible
decision without exposing evidence or allowing popularity to pressure him.

| Stage | Ibrahim's question | Product response | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triage | “Is this safe, complete, and in scope?” | Structured queue with privacy and conflict warnings. | Unsafe exposure is contained quickly. |
| Investigate | “What supports or contradicts the claim?” | Provenance, permissions, confirmations, evidence, and duplicate tools. | He sees relevant context without excess data. |
| Decide | “Which status and reason are justified?” | Policy-linked decisions and required rationale. | The decision is consistent and auditable. |
| Communicate | “What can I safely explain?” | Separate private notes from public explanation. | Users understand the result without leaked data. |
| Appeal | “Can another reviewer reassess this?” | Preserved history and appeal workflow. | Correction does not erase the original decision. |

**Main failure to avoid:** opaque or irreversible moderation presented as
validation.

## Journey 5: Nneka's organization supports a mission

**Context:** Nneka represents a school, NGO, business, or community group with
expertise, testers, equipment, or implementation capacity.

| Stage | Nneka's question | Product response | Success signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | “Does this align with our mandate?” | Filterable verified missions and clear affected-user context. | She finds credible opportunities. |
| Assess | “What evidence, risk, and commitment exist?” | Reviewed context, constraints, proposals, team, and milestones. | She can conduct due diligence. |
| Support | “How can we help without taking over?” | Defined roles, transparent offers, conflict disclosure, and community stewardship. | Support strengthens community ownership. |
| Track | “What happened with our support?” | Milestones, testing, implementation evidence, and impact level. | The organization can report outcomes honestly. |

**Main failure to avoid:** organizational visibility or funding overriding the
community's needs and decision process.

## Shared trust moments

The product must be especially clear when a user:

- Chooses what becomes public.
- Distinguishes confirmation from support.
- Reads a validation or moderation decision.
- Sees a verified badge or mission-ready status.
- Grants repository or provider access.
- Reviews generated Solution Lab output.
- Encounters an implementation or impact claim.

These moments should receive explicit copy, accessible design, authorization
tests, and user-research attention.
